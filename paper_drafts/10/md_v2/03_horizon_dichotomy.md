# 3. Finite-radius zeros of the family

The parameter (\lambda) first enters the weak-field metric at second order, but its influence is not limited to a small perturbative correction. It also determines whether the static time coefficient vanishes at a finite radius.

The family derived in Sec. 2 is

[
A_\lambda(r)
============

\left(
1+\lambda\frac{r_s}{r}
\right)^{-1/\lambda},
\qquad
B_\lambda(r)=A_\lambda(r)^{-1},
]

with the continuous limiting member

[
A_0(r)=e^{-r_s/r}.
]

For (\lambda<0), the factor in parentheses vanishes when

[
1+\lambda\frac{r_s}{r}=0.
]

The corresponding radius is

[
\boxed{
r_0=-\lambda r_s
}.
]

Since (r_s>0), this radius is positive precisely when (\lambda<0). Therefore,

[
\boxed{
\lambda<0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
A_\lambda(r)
\text{ has a finite positive-radius zero}
}.
]

For (\lambda\geq0), the metric function remains positive for every finite (r>0). In particular,

[
A_0(r)=e^{-r_s/r}>0
]

and, for (\lambda>0),

[
1+\lambda\frac{r_s}{r}>0.
]

The Schwarzschild member corresponds to

[
\lambda=-1,
]

for which

[
A_{-1}(r)
=========

1-\frac{r_s}{r}.
]

Its zero occurs at

[
r_0=r_s
]

and is the standard Schwarzschild horizon.

For other negative values of (\lambda), the family likewise contains a finite-radius zero at (r_0=-\lambda r_s). This paper does not classify the local regularity or global causal character of those surfaces and refers to them only as zeros of the static time coefficient.

The weak-field expansion is

[
A_\lambda(r)
============

1-\frac{r_s}{r}
+
\frac{1+\lambda}{2}
\left(
\frac{r_s}{r}
\right)^2
+
O\!\left(
\frac{r_s^3}{r^3}
\right).
]

The parameter (\lambda) controls both the first nonlinear weak-field correction and the existence of a finite-radius zero. The assumptions of Sec. 2 leave it undetermined.

# Abstract

Simple derivations of the Schwarzschild metric from energy and Newtonian arguments have been proposed and disputed for decades. We ask what a common set of such assumptions actually determines. For a static, spherically symmetric, asymptotically flat exterior, we impose the Newtonian limit, an exact vacuum flux law in a monotonic potential variable, (g_{tt}g_{rr}=-1), and a constant nonlinear self-source coefficient. These assumptions yield, and establish the completeness of, a one-parameter family whose closed form has appeared previously:

[
A_\lambda(r)
============

\left(1+\lambda\frac{r_s}{r}\right)^{-1/\lambda},
\qquad
r_s=\frac{2GM}{c^2},
]

with (B_\lambda=A_\lambda^{-1}) and Schwarzschild recovered at (\lambda=-1). All members share the same Newtonian limit and the post-Newtonian value (\gamma=1), while the nonlinear parameter satisfies

[
\beta=\lambda+2.
]

Thus measurements of (\beta) select the Schwarzschild member even though the static assumptions do not. The sign of (\lambda) also determines whether (A_\lambda) has a finite positive-radius zero; for (\lambda=-1), this zero is the Schwarzschild horizon. Within a specified class of gradient-local static energy ledgers, a two-shell assembly calculation confines (\lambda) to ([-1/4,1/4]) and therefore excludes the Schwarzschild value. Finally, in Painlevé–Gullstrand coordinates, the Schwarzschild member is uniquely characterized by the exact Newtonian infall relation (v^2=2GM/r), explaining Michell’s radius without treating the escape-velocity argument as a derivation of the metric. The shared assumptions therefore determine the scale and the family; additional physical or observational input selects the member.

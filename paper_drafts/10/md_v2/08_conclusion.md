# 8. Conclusion

Under the stated static, spherically symmetric assumptions, the exterior metric is determined as the complete one-parameter family

[
\boxed{
A_\lambda(r)
============

\left(
1+\lambda\frac{r_s}{r}
\right)^{-1/\lambda}
},
\qquad
B_\lambda(r)=A_\lambda(r)^{-1},
]

with

[
r_s=\frac{2GM}{c^2}.
]

The assumptions fix the mass scale, the Newtonian limit, and the relation between the temporal and radial metric coefficients. They do not fix the nonlinear parameter (\lambda).

Although the closed metric form has appeared previously, the present analysis derives the continuous family as the complete outcome of the stated assumptions and separates family construction from member selection.

The Schwarzschild exterior is the member

[
\lambda=-1.
]

A finite positive-radius zero of (A_\lambda) exists precisely when (\lambda<0); for the Schwarzschild member, that zero occurs at (r_s) and is the standard Schwarzschild horizon.

Within the static bookkeeping class examined in Sec. 4, the assembly ledger confines (\lambda) to

[
-\frac14\leq\lambda\leq\frac14
]

and therefore does not select Schwarzschild.

The invariant assembly energy remains correct: the conditional tension concerns its localization and division between the matter and field columns, not energy conservation itself.

The post-Newtonian transformation gives

[
\boxed{
\gamma=1,
\qquad
\beta=\lambda+2
}.
]

The family parameter is therefore identified observationally by

[
\lambda=\beta-2.
]

The value (\beta\simeq1) selects (\lambda\simeq-1), and hence the Schwarzschild member.

For that member, the Painlevé–Gullstrand shift satisfies

[
\frac12v^2=\frac{GM}{r}
]

exactly. This explains why Michell’s escape-velocity calculation reproduces the Schwarzschild radius.

The result is therefore neither a unique static derivation of Schwarzschild nor a proof that simple assumptions yield nothing. The assumptions determine a complete family. Additional physical or observational information selects its Schwarzschild member.

# 3. The horizon dichotomy: what rides on the parameter

Before asking what fixes $\lambda$, it is worth establishing what
hangs on it. The answer is not a correction but a structure.

The function $A_\lambda = (1 + \lambda x)^{-1/\lambda}$ vanishes at
finite radius if and only if $1 + \lambda x$ has a positive root with
the exponent driving $A$ to zero there — that is, if and only if
$\lambda < 0$, with the root at

$$r_{\rm h} = -\lambda\, r_s .$$

At $\lambda = -1$ this is the Schwarzschild horizon at $r_s$ exactly.
For $\lambda \geq 0$ there is no root: the exponential member
$e^{-x}$ and the reciprocal member $(1+x)^{-1}$ are positive for all
$r > 0$. Clocks slow without limit as $r \to 0$, but no surface of
infinite redshift forms at any finite radius.

So the family does not spread the horizon around; it removes it. For
half the parameter line — including the members closest to innocent
expectation, the one that counts no self-sourcing at all and the ones
that count it with positive sign — there is no horizon anywhere.
Miscounting the field's energy does not relocate the surface $A = 0$;
it deletes it. The choice of member is therefore not a refinement at
second order. The existence of black-hole horizons rides on it, and
so does the meaning of the paper's title: if energy accounting could
deliver the Schwarzschild radius in the sense that matters — a radius
at which something happens — it would be delivering horizon existence
from Newtonian-grade reasoning. Whether it can is the question of the
next section, and the answer will be no; which measurement can is the
question of the section after that.

The phenomenon itself — that an alternative treatment of gravitational
field energy can abolish horizons — is not new. It is the most
conspicuous feature of the Yilmaz theory, which modifies general
relativity precisely by counting the field's stress-energy as an
explicit source and yields an exponential metric with no horizons;
the point was argued at length in the exchange between Misner (1999)
and Alley and Yilmaz (1999), and its astrophysical stakes were
developed by Robertson (1997). Within our family the exponential
appears as the $\lambda \to 0$ member, and the resemblance should be
handled with care: Yilmaz's exponential metric is exponential in
*isotropic* form, with $g_{ij} = e^{2\Phi_{\rm iso}}\delta_{ij}$ and
$\beta = 1$, whereas the $\lambda = 0$ member here is exponential in
the areal time component with $B = 1/A$, and has $\beta = 2$ (Sec. 5).
They are different metrics making different solar-system predictions;
what they share is the moral. What the family adds to the folklore is
the theorem form: horizon existence is a dichotomy across the entire
counting-weight line, not a property of one alternative theory
considered in isolation — and, once the dictionary of Sec. 5 is in
hand, a dichotomy tied to a measured number.

# Does the universe expand by stretching its vacuum cells, or by growing more of them?

Cosmic expansion has two readings consistent with the same a(t) history — one of them is dead, twice over.

## The kill

The closed metric sector cannot distinguish "space stretches" from "space grows": both a fixed
graph with dilating cells (l = l₀a(t), cell count N fixed) and a fixed-scale graph that adds
cells (l = const, N ~ a³) reproduce the same a³ volume history and the same dust stress-energy.
The discriminator isn't the metric — it's what each reading does to quantities the theory has
already locked elsewhere. Both locks say stretch is dead.

**Kill 1 — G drifts, and drifts too fast.** The floor-Newton lock (derivation 039) ties
Newton's constant to the microstructural edge length: G ~ 1/(f′(Δ₀) l). If l stretches with
the scale factor, Ġ/G = −H:

```text
stretch prediction:  Ġ/G ~ −7.2e-11 /yr
LLR bound:            |Ġ/G| < 1.5e-13 /yr
excluded by ~480x
```

**Kill 2 — the floor turns into overclosing dust.** The wedge energies that make up the floor
are exactly dilation-invariant (locked in derivation 038, measured in 041 E3). Stretch cells
and the floor's total energy E stays fixed while volume V grows, so p = −dE/dV = 0: pressureless,
w = 0. A w=0 component isn't sequestered — P3 only protects *constant density*, and stretched-floor
density falls as u ~ a⁻³, not flat. A Planck-density (~5×10¹¹² J/m³) pressureless relic would
overclose the universe by roughly 10¹²² — the same absurd number that makes the raw cosmological
constant problem famous, arrived at from the opposite direction.

Either failure alone kills stretch; together they kill it independently twice, with no shared
assumption between the two death certificates. The survivor is growth: l stays fixed (so G is
exactly constant, forever — new register entry A6), the floor's intensive energy density u is
constant (measured, not assumed, in 051 E1), giving p = −u exactly, w = −1 at the substance
level, and the first law funds new-cell creation term by term with zero free parameter. Space
grows by adding cells; it does not stretch existing ones.

# How far does the unwanted scalaron reach?

The packing's R^2-class term, once reconstructed by Bianchi, predicts a massive scalar field — here is its range.

## The number

```text
l* = sqrt(6 alpha) ~ sqrt(6) a ~= 4e-35 m   (at Planck packing, a ~ l_P)
```

[The Bianchi reconstruction](thm_scalaron_reconstruction.md) shows that P7' ("the four-derivative
sector is exactly empty") is a continuum statement, a -> 0. At finite packing scale a, the
wedge-energy function f(Delta) carries an f'' term, giving an effective f(R) = R + alpha R^2
with alpha ~ f''(Delta_0) a^2. That alpha is not free: it is fixed by the same wedge-energy
coefficient that sets the floor density and the Newton lock, and it comes with a mass,
m^2 = 1/(6 alpha), and a Yukawa range, l* = sqrt(6 alpha).

Because alpha scales as a^2, the range scales linearly in the packing constant:

- l* ~ sqrt(6) a, same order as the packing scale itself.
- At Planck packing (a ~ l_P), l* ~= 4e-35 m.
- The current laboratory frontier for a gravitational-strength Yukawa deviation is ~54
  micrometers (sub-mm torsion-balance tests of the inverse-square law).
- Ratio: ~1e30. Ten orders of magnitude short of merely small — thirty orders below any
  apparatus that exists or is proposed.

This is the theory-owner's July 2026 scoping ruling (derivation 048, Route i): rather than
hunt for a mechanism to kill the scalaron outright (Route ii required f''(Delta_0) = 0, an
unmotivated inflection condition on the wedge energy — rejected, kept on record as fallback),
P7' is simply rescoped as an idealization. The physical vacuum carries exactly two derived,
controlled, sub-observable corrections to the static shadow: the expansion leak
(see [The Kappa Leak](num_kappa_leak.md)) and this discreteness leak. Both are quadratic in
their small parameter; neither reopens a closed field-equation coefficient; neither is
observable at any accessible range. A third correction, if one is ever found, must arrive the
same way — derived, with a magnitude and a kill condition, never assumed away.

The falsifier face this creates is sharp and parameter-free: if the packing scale a were ever
independently measured by any discreteness probe, the theory predicts a scalaron Yukawa at
range sqrt(6) a with a specific wedge-energy coefficient — a consistency test between two
would-be discoveries, not a free parameter to fit after the fact.

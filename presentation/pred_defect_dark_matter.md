# If dark matter is a packing defect, will anyone ever detect it directly?

A conditional prediction (B2 in the register): not a claim that defects *are* dark matter, but a claim about what follows if they are.

## The claim

The packing has a candidate dark-matter object already sitting in the theory, unlicensed: **coordination defects** — rings with n ≠ 5 tetrahedra, relaxed and locally stable, carrying quantized positive excess strain over the five-fold floor (E4: 0.0605, E6: 0.0517 lab units; derivation 041 E4). They're persistent, localized, quantized, and gravitating — non-constant energy density, so visible to the trace-free dynamics the way the sequestered floor is not. That is exactly the phenomenological profile the dark-excess lane needs for a w ≈ 0 candidate.

But the object is not the dark sector. P10's fence is explicit: a defect is a candidate *geometric* structure. Turning it into a dark-matter *candidate* requires production physics, an abundance calculation, and clustering phenomenology under the P9 fence — none of which is licensed yet (blocked on O-P10-5: do disclination networks form spontaneously, with what spectrum, at what density?).

The conditional prediction is what falls out once you grant the identification anyway, because defects are strain structures of the packing and carry no standard-model charge by construction:

```text
IF defect excess = dark matter
THEN: DM couples to ordinary matter through geometry (gravity) only —
      no electromagnetic, weak, or new-force coupling of any kind.
      Direct-detection experiments (WIMP nuclear recoil, axion
      haloscopes, etc.) find nothing. Not "not yet." Forever.
      DM phenomenology is cold and collisionless at leading order.
```

This is a purity claim, not a mass or cross-section prediction — the theory doesn't yet have those (that's C2, gated on the same O-P10-5 obligation, plus 017–019). What it has now is the coupling structure: geometry in, geometry out, nothing else.

The kill condition runs the other direction from most of this register's entries. A confirmed non-gravitational DM detection — a WIMP recoil, an axion signal, anything coupling DM to the standard model outside gravity — does not touch the theory. It kills the *candidate*. The vacuum sector, Lambda, the closed field equations, sequestering: none of it depends on defects being dark matter. Lose this candidate and the theory loses a speculative extra chapter, not a load-bearing pillar.

What makes this entry unusual in the register: four decades of null direct-detection results (WIMP searches, axion haloscopes, and everything between) are ordinary bad news for most of the field's dark-matter program. Here, continued nulls are mildly *confirmatory* — they're what a geometry-only coupling predicts, forever, by construction. This is the rare prediction where the absence of a signal is doing real epistemic work for the candidate rather than just failing to rule it out.

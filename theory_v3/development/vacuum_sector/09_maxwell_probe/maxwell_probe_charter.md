## The Question

The vacuum is a physical substance whose configuration supplies
spacetime geometry. At the microscopic level, the model's vertices are
**spatial quanta with Delaunay adjacency**. Edges carry lengths, and
those lengths fix the geometry of each tetrahedron. The tetrahedra are
how the model represents the geometry among neighboring quanta
([P2](../../../01_postulates/p2_vacuum_spacetime_identity.md),
[P10](../../../01_postulates/p10_packing_axiom.md)).

The research question:

```text
At fixed macroscopic metric, does a periodic, non-inverting
configuration generated by VED's strain model leave a reproducible
directional signature in Maxwell modes after averaging?
```

Both outcomes are informative.

- **A signal** would mean the arrangement of spatial quanta carries
  directional information that survives averaging and reaches an
  electromagnetic mode.
- **A null** would show how local angle-gap strain can store energy
  while the modeled large-scale optical response stays isotropic.

## Two Quantities to Keep Distinct

```text
Delta_0  = 2 pi - 5 arccos(1/3) ~ 7.36 deg
           the mismatch of the PREFERRED arrangement: five regular
           Euclidean tetrahedra around an edge leave this gap

delta_h  = 2 pi - (sum of the ACTUAL incident dihedral angles at h)
           the Regge deficit of an actual configuration
```

- On the regular 600-cell, the cells are regular, so delta_h = Delta_0
  at every hinge. Curvature absorbs the gap and there is no strain.
- In a valid flat periodic embedding, delta_h = 0 at every interior
  edge, yet the configuration can carry positive strain energy. That
  energy is the cost of the quanta departing from their preferred
  geometry so that the arrangement closes.

Code must compute these two quantities separately and must never
report one under the name of the other. A strain variable built only
from delta_h reads zero in a flat configuration however strained it is.

## What Maxwell Is Here

Discrete Maxwell theory is placed on the edge geometry as a test field:

```text
E  1-cochain on edges        B  2-cochain on faces
d1^T *2 d1 e = (omega/c)^2 *1 e
```

The Hodge stars `*1`, `*2` carry all of the geometric input. That
makes them the place where an interpretive choice enters the probe.

### The Hodge-star fork (`hodge_star_interpretive_fork`)

```text
(a) actual-geometry star   circumcentric (signed) or Whitney weights
                           built from the actual edge lengths.
                           The default. It probes whether the actual
                           arrangement of quanta leaves a signature.

(b) mismatch-dependent     a constitutive star that depends on the
    star                   preferred-vs-actual mismatch. A VED
                           interpretive choice, not defined here; the
                           theory owner's to make.
```

The expected property of (a): uniform E and B are exact discrete
static solutions on any valid embedded mesh with signed circumcentric
weights.

- Gauss: d0^T *1 e at a vertex is E dotted into the closed dual cell's
  area vectors.
- Faraday dual: d1^T *2 b at an edge is B dotted into the closed loop
  of circumcenters around it.

So under (a) the long-wavelength rank-2 response is the macroscopic
metric, and any signature must appear at finite k. This is recorded as
an **expected property to verify** (gates G3a and G3b below), not as a
result. It is a property of choice (a). A null obtained under (a) is
not a verdict on VED.

The two branches answer different questions and should be run on the
same packing:

1. **Fork (a) first**, with ordinary vacuum Maxwell coefficients. This
   measures how much apparent anisotropy the mesh and solver produce on
   their own.
2. **Fork (b) second**, once a coupling between the angle-gap strain and
   the electromagnetic response has been specified. This asks what
   additional signal that coupling produces.

If the (b)−(a) difference persists at wavelengths much longer than the
spacing between spatial quanta, and across independent packings, it is
a candidate long-wavelength prediction of that coupling. If it fades,
the run gives the scale at which microscopic strain becomes optically
invisible. Fork (a) is a computable baseline. Fork (b) adds a physical
coupling to VED.

Acceptance checks:

```text
counts     upstairs 120 / 720 / 1200 / 600; quotient 1 / 6 / 10 / 5
action     I* acts on the LEFT; edge class = Im(conj(q_i) q_j)
           (invariant under simultaneous left multiplication)
smooth     omega R / c = 2 (analytic; the curl sign -2/R is
           checked by Stokes circulation in a right-handed frame,
           not by the discrete operator)
discrete   lowest triplet omega R / c = 2.10292445
           second triplet omega R / c = 8.90813092
pattern    the sampled frame patterns span the lowest eigenspace
symmetry   the A5 character of the signed edge representation is
           (6, -2, 0, 1, 1) = chi_3 + chi_3'
control    a 1% stretch of one edge class splits the low triplet
           (reference: 2.08334853 x2, 2.13210754)
```

The quotient is a one-vertex triangulation with face identifications.
All six edges are loops, which an ordinary abstract simplicial-complex
data structure cannot represent. The scratch code works upstairs on
all 720 edges and classes them by `Im(conj(q_i) q_j)`. The forge
version should either do the same or use a cell-complex structure that
allows loops.

**What 056 does not validate.** The Poincaré sphere has H¹ = 0, so the
regular quotient has no harmonic modes. 056 exercises a known
oscillating field and its frequency. It cannot exercise the uniform
fields that wrap around a periodic box. The torus checks (G3a–G3c) are
the complementary test and must be passed separately before stage 4.

### Stage 3 — `p10_wedge_strain_definition`, then the P10 hinge run

**Wedge step SATISFIED by forge 059**

E_h = (K/2) Σ (θ_i − arccos(1/3))² per hinge, K explicit and not

represent. Counting all six dihedrals of each tetrahedron raises the
wedge energy about 1.2×, so the bulk form sums over every
(tetrahedron, edge) dihedral. The bulk run is
`p10_bulk_relaxation_059`.

This stage answers the VED question with the P10 energy rather than the
spring proxy. Before any bulk run:

1. Write down how the preferred angle gap Delta_0 produces a stored
   deformation in the chosen f. In a periodic flat embedding the actual
   deficit is zero at every valid interior edge, so f must see the
   mismatch through the actual cell geometry, not through delta_h
   alone.
2. Verify it on a single five-tetrahedron wedge. The spring-model
   reference is 041 E2: the relaxed n = 5 ring has E = 0.000649.

Only then go to bulk. Otherwise stages 2 and 3 could differ for an
accidental reason. The choice of f is the theory owner's.

The mechanical question for this stage: does the angle gap produce a
persistent directional pattern in where the energy is stored? The
comparison that counts is with the stage-2 spring control under the
same conditions. Positive stored energy alone does not imply a
preferred direction.

This question can be answered before any electromagnetic coupling is
chosen, and its answer stands on its own.

- Start from matched initial packings, and run both energy models
  across several seeds and box sizes.
- Measure the strength of the directional anisotropy **within each
  packing** with a rotation-invariant quantity, for example the norm of
  the deviatoric strain-energy fabric, before averaging across seeds. A
  preferred axis could rotate from sample to sample and cancel in a
  box-axis average.
- Record each packing's principal axes, so stage 4 can ask whether the
  fastest and slowest propagation directions line up with them.

If the angle-gap model shows no anisotropy beyond the spring control,
polarization splitting becomes a weaker target, and a speed shift
shared by both polarizations may be the more informative observable.

### Stage 4 — `maxwell_packing_sweep`

Started in forge 058
([periodic_maxwell_calibration_vacuumforge.md](periodic_maxwell_calibration_vacuumforge.md)):
on the 057 packing, the signed circumcentric weights are no longer
positive after relaxation (the Delaunay property is lost), so the E2
rule selects **Whitney mass matrices** for this and all paired
comparisons. G3a–G3c pass. Fork (a) recovers c along [100], [110] and
[111] as ka → 0, with quadratic finite-ka departures and a small
polarization splitting: the baseline. The ensemble, the P10 packings,
and fork (b) remain.

Send the Maxwell probe through the stage-2 and stage-3 packings.

**At k = 0 (calibration).** A connected three-torus mesh has V − 1
independent gradients plus three independent curl-free classes, one
for each way around the box, so the nullity of d₁ is V − 1 + 3. A
solver can return any mixture of these zero modes, so the tests (G3a,
G3b) remove the gradient subspace and compare subspaces rather than
individual vectors.

**At k ≠ 0 (Bloch scan).** A periodic box of side L only allows
k = 2πn/L, which is coarse near zero. Bloch-twisted boundary
conditions (cochains pick up the phase e^{ik·L} across each box face)
let k be scanned continuously on one box. Under a nontrivial twist:

- the three static harmonic classes disappear: the twisted cohomology
  vanishes;
- the gradient subspace gains one dimension, from V − 1 to V, because
  the twisted d₀ has no kernel;
- the two low-frequency transverse branches are the ones to track.

**Observable.** Measure the phase speed ω/|k|, in units of c, for each
polarization along several directions, as a function of ka, where a is
the packing spacing. A raw frequency difference shrinks toward zero
simply because both frequencies do; the inferred speeds show whether a
fractional optical difference survives. In homogeneous flat vacuum both
polarizations approach c in every direction, which gives the solver a
clear reference.

Track two quantities, because they can behave differently:

- **the mean speed** of the two polarizations, against direction and
  ka. Both polarizations can shift together. Strain would then have an
  optical effect even with zero splitting.
- **the polarization splitting.** If the splitting weakens as the
  wavelength grows while the mean shift persists, light has lost
  sensitivity to the direction of the microscopic strain but remains
  sensitive to its average effect.

**Alignment.** Where stage 3 finds a per-packing anisotropy, test
whether the fastest and slowest propagation directions line up with its
principal axes. Agreement across independent packings says
considerably more than a split frequency on one mesh.

**Reading a positive outcome.** It has two levels. First, VED's packing
mechanics predicts a strain pattern (stage 3, standing on its own).
Second, a specified electromagnetic coupling makes that pattern
optically visible (stage 4, fork (b) − fork (a)). The second gives the
combined model something concrete to test.

**Scope of a Bloch result.** It describes that particular packing
repeated periodically. Changing box size and packing realization tests
how much the repetition matters (G5).

**What to watch.** Whether degeneracies survive, whether speeds shift
or split by polarization, and which changes persist after averaging
across independently generated packings. Run fork (a) first; run fork
(b) on the same packings once a coupling is specified.
